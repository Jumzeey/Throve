# PRODUCT.md

## Throve Product Definition

**Document status:** Final  
**Last updated:** 1 August 2026  
**Current product name:** Throve

---

## 1. Product summary

Throve is a **mobile-first social fashion resale marketplace with live-shopping features**.

The aim is to combine:

- Buying and selling fashion and beauty items, both new and pre-owned.
- A social, engaging shopping experience.
- Live-shopping experiences where products can be presented and purchased.

Throve is currently the working product name. Whether Throve will remain the final brand name is **To be decided**.

---

## 2. Current product stage

Throve is at the beginning of the build process.

The immediate goal is to create a **first working prototype**. The prototype will be used to demonstrate the idea and test whether the main experience works.

The first prototype must not automatically be treated as a production-ready application.

A production-ready application would require additional work, testing and specialist review, particularly for:

- User accounts and authentication.
- Payments.
- Seller payouts.
- Database permissions.
- Private messages.
- Personal user data.
- Security and privacy.

No product feature is currently marked as completed because no test results, screenshots or direct user-testing evidence have yet been recorded.

---

## 3. Confirmed decisions

The following decisions have been confirmed through the available project conversations.

### Product direction

- The product is called **Throve** for now.
- Throve will focus on fashion and beauty resale.
- Throve will support new, unused and pre-owned items.
- Throve will be designed primarily for mobile use.
- Throve will include social marketplace elements.
- Live shopping is part of the overall product idea.
- The initial launch country for Throve is **Nigeria**.
- The platform will be **one shared application for both iPhone and Android**.
- The first prototype will contain real user accounts.
- Sellers will be responsible for shipping purchased items to buyers.

### Build approach

- The project will be built with extensive AI assistance.
- Codex and other AI agents may be used to help build and review the product.
- The founder does not have a coding background.
- Instructions and project tasks must therefore be written in clear, non-technical language.
- The founder has chosen to begin building without completing a separate product-validation stage first.
- Skipping formal product validation does not mean that user testing should be skipped once a prototype exists.
- The project must be built in small, controlled steps.
- Agent claims alone are not evidence that work is complete.
- Completed work must be supported by testing, screenshots, a working preview or direct user testing.

## 4. Intended users

Throve’s main target customers are:

### Buyers

Individuals who want to:

- Buy pre-owned, used or unused personal fashion and beauty items.
- Save money through thrifting.
- Discover and view fashion and beauty listings.
- Save or favourite items.
- Contact sellers.
- Make offers.
- Purchase items.

### Okrika sellers

Okrika sellers who want to:

- List and sell fashion items.
- Reach more potential buyers.
- Present and promote their products.
- Communicate with interested buyers.
- Potentially sell through live-shopping sessions.

### Individual sellers

Individuals who want to:

- Sell used or unused personal fashion and beauty items.
- Make back some of the money originally spent on those items.
- List, present and promote items.
- Communicate with interested buyers.
- Receive and respond to offers.

### Live-shopping hosts

A seller may also act as a live-shopping host.

Initially, only invited sellers will be able to host live sessions during the controlled live phase. After meeting required conditions, all accounts may eventually be able to host live sessions.

Whether live-shopping hosts will have a separate account type or additional requirements is **To be decided**.

---

## 5. The problem Throve intends to address

Throve is intended to make fashion and beauty resale feel more social and engaging than a traditional listings-only marketplace.

The current concept brings together:

- New, unused and pre-owned fashion and beauty items.
- Okrika and second-hand fashion in Nigeria.
- Affordable thrifting.
- A way for individuals to recover some money from items they no longer need.
- Social discovery.
- Buyer and seller communication.
- Seller-led content.
- Live product presentation.

The main target customers are confirmed. However, the strongest reason users will choose Throve instead of an existing marketplace is still **To be decided** and should be tested through prototype feedback rather than treated as a proven fact.

## 6. First prototype objective

The purpose of the first prototype is to show that the main Throve concept can work as a simple mobile experience.

The prototype should help answer:

1. Can a user understand what Throve is?
2. Can a user create and use a real account?
3. Can a user discover fashion and beauty items?
4. Can a seller present an item for sale?
5. Can a buyer save or favourite an item?
6. Can buyers and sellers message each other?
7. Can a buyer make an offer that the seller can accept or reject?
8. Can a seller send an offer to a potential buyer who favourited an item?
9. Does the product feel social rather than like a basic classified-ad website?
10. Can the live-shopping idea be demonstrated clearly?
11. Can a user move through the main experience without becoming confused?

The exact full list of screens for the first prototype is **To be decided**.

## 7. First prototype scope

The first prototype should represent the following confirmed parts of the product.

### A. Mobile-first experience

The experience should be designed for a phone-sized screen first.

Desktop support may be added later, but it should not control the initial design.

### B. Initial categories

The first supported categories will be:

- Clothing.
- Bags.
- Shoes.
- Accessories.
- Beauty products.

Items may be listed for different customer groups, including women, men and children.

All items may be listed in different conditions, including:

- New with tags.
- New without tags.
- Very good.
- Good.
- Satisfactory.

This includes brand-new, unused and pre-owned items.

### C. Marketplace experience

The prototype should demonstrate how a user discovers and views items.

The confirmed marketplace functions include:

- Viewing product listings.
- Opening an individual product listing.
- Liking or favouriting an item.
- Viewing the seller connected to an item.

The following remain **To be decided**:

- The exact product-feed design.
- Search functions.
- Category navigation.
- Filters and sorting.
- The full seller-profile design.

### D. Seller listing experience

Each listing must include:

- Photos.
- A title.
- A description.
- Item details:
  - Category.
  - Brand.
  - Condition.
- Pricing:
  - Item price.
- Shipping information.

The exact number of photos, description limits, required shipping fields and optional listing fields are **To be decided**.

### E. Social experience

The following social features are confirmed for the first prototype:

- Users can like or add listings to their favourites.
- Buyers can message sellers.
- Buyers can make offers that sellers can accept or reject.
- Sellers can send buy-now offers to potential buyers who have favourited their items.
- Sellers can message potential buyers who have favourited their items.
- Users can comment during live sessions.
- Individual product listings will not have public comment sections.

Because private messaging and real accounts involve personal user data, these features must receive technical review and evidence-based testing.

### F. Live-shopping experience

The first prototype will use **real live video**.

Live sessions will initially be restricted to invited sellers only during the controlled phase.

Later, all accounts may be able to host live sessions after meeting required conditions.

How live shopping will be fully implemented beyond the prototype is **To be decided**.

## 8. Account, purchase, shipping and refund approach

### Real accounts

The first prototype will contain **real user accounts**, not simulated accounts.

Accounts are expected to support buying, selling, saving items, messaging and live-shopping interaction.

The exact sign-up, login, account-recovery and identity-verification requirements are **To be decided**.

Authentication, account recovery, database permissions and personal user data are high-risk areas and require technical review.

### Purchases in the prototype

The first prototype will include a purchase journey without real money exchange.

This means users may go through the buying process, but:

- No real payment will be collected.
- No real seller payout will take place.
- The prototype may record or display a simulated completed purchase.

The exact simulated purchase steps are **To be decided**.

### Shipping in the real system

Sellers will ship purchased items to buyers.

The following shipping details are **To be decided**:

- Which courier services will be supported.
- Whether sellers can choose their own courier.
- How delivery charges will be calculated.
- Whether the buyer or seller pays for delivery.
- Delivery times.
- Tracking requirements.
- What evidence counts as proof of dispatch and delivery.

### Damaged, false or undelivered items

The confirmed product rule is:

- The buyer will receive a full refund after providing acceptable evidence.
- A damaged, false or misrepresented item will be returned to the seller.
- An undelivered item cannot be returned because the buyer never received it.

The following details remain **To be decided**:

- What evidence the buyer must provide.
- The time limit for reporting a problem.
- Who pays return-shipping costs.
- The required return method.
- Who reviews and decides disputes.
- When refund money is released.
- What happens if the buyer and seller disagree.

## 9. Features not automatically included in the first prototype

The following features must not be added to the first prototype unless they are separately approved:

- Real payment processing.
- Seller payouts.
- Production-level identity checks.
- Push notifications.
- Ratings and reviews through a post-transaction feedback system.
- Automated returns.
- A full buyer or seller dispute system.
- Shipping-label creation.
- Automated delivery tracking.
- Advanced search.
- Personalised recommendations.
- Complex seller analytics.
- Listing boosts that involve real payments.
- Shipping services operated by Throve.
- Subscription plans.
- Loyalty or reward systems.
- International buying and selling.
- Multiple currencies.
- Multiple languages.
- A full moderation system.
- A full customer-support system.
- Separate native iPhone and Android applications beyond the shared-app approach.
- Production-quality live-video infrastructure beyond prototype needs.

Some of these may be required before a public launch, but they are not confirmed as first-prototype requirements.

## 14. Prototype success criteria

The first prototype will not be considered successful only because it opens or looks attractive.

It should meet the following minimum standards:

- The main purpose of Throve is understandable.
- Real users can create and access accounts.
- The key mobile screens work together as one journey.
- A user can discover and view fashion or beauty items.
- A seller can create a listing with the required information.
- A user can like or favourite an item.
- A buyer can message a seller.
- A buyer can make an offer.
- A seller can accept or reject an offer.
- A seller can message or send an offer to a potential buyer who favourited an item.
- The live-shopping experience is clearly demonstrated using real video.
- Important buttons and navigation work.
- Relevant loading, empty and error states have been considered.
- There are no obvious broken screens.
- The prototype has been tested on a phone-sized screen.
- Screenshots or recordings exist as evidence.
- The founder has personally tested the main journeys.
- Known problems and unfinished areas are recorded.

The final evidence required before the prototype is approved is **To be decided**.

## 15. Production-readiness rule

A working prototype is not automatically a production-ready product.

Before Throve handles real users, private information or money, it will require additional checks.

High-risk areas include:

- Authentication.
- Account recovery.
- Database permissions.
- Personal information.
- User-uploaded photos and content.
- Private messages.
- Payment processing.
- Seller payouts.
- Refunds and disputes.
- Platform moderation.
- Legal and privacy requirements.

These areas require technical review and evidence before public launch.

## 17. Scope-control rule

New features should not be added simply because they are suggested by an AI agent or because another marketplace has them.

A feature should only enter the first prototype when:

- It supports the main Throve concept.
- It is needed for the agreed user journey.
- Its risks and dependencies are understood.
- It has a clear definition of done.
- It has been deliberately approved.

Everything else should remain in a later-version list.

## 18. Current product statement

Throve is a mobile-first social fashion and beauty resale marketplace with live-shopping features.

Its main target customers are:

- Individuals who want to save money by buying pre-owned, used or unused personal fashion and beauty items.
- Okrika sellers.
- Individuals who want to sell used or unused personal fashion and beauty items and recover some of the money they spent.

The initial categories are clothing, bags, shoes, accessories and beauty products.

The initial launch market is Nigeria, and the platform will be one shared application for iPhone and Android.

The first prototype will use real accounts and will include listings, favourites, buyer and seller messaging, buyer offers, seller responses and seller offers to interested users. It will also demonstrate real live video.

In the real system, sellers will ship purchased items to buyers. Buyers will be entitled to a full refund for damaged, false, misrepresented or undelivered items after providing acceptable evidence.

Throve plans to earn money through buyer protection fees, payment-processing charges, optional paid seller listing boosts and shipping services at a later stage.

The full live-shopping model, prototype approval evidence, first public-version scope and final brand name are still **To be decided**.
